## Problem Statement

CallBridge needs a repeatable, judge-visible end-to-end call that proves the
complete WebMCP-to-phone-to-evidence loop. Calling a real hotel for every judge
run would disturb staff, make debugging unpredictable, introduce privacy and
consent problems, and leave the final demo dependent on a stranger answering at
the right time.

A purely scripted browser simulation would be reliable but would weaken the
product's central claim. The important proof is not that two text agents can
exchange messages. It is that ChatGPT can prepare an authenticated task through
WebMCP, a person can approve one exact call on the webpage, CallBridge can cross
the real telephone network, and the answer can return as evidence rather than a
fabricated summary.

## What Makes This Cool

The judge can ask an original question, not select a prepared script. ChatGPT
turns it into a visible call plan, the judge confirms it, and CallBridge calls a
real phone number. On the other end, a clearly disclosed AI receptionist for a
fictional hotel answers from a fixed, testable fact sheet. The calling agent must
listen, handle interruption, avoid repeating resolved questions,
and return a result whose evidence can be compared with what the receptionist
actually said.

This creates a live controlled laboratory without turning the product into a
hotel app. The product remains a general inquiry caller. “Aurora Demo Hotel” is
only the safe recipient used to prove it.

## Constraints

- The feature must strengthen the hackathon story without replacing the general
  inquiry contract with a hotel-specific product.
- The call must traverse a real telephony route. A local function call, generated
  transcript, or browser animation does not count as the primary proof.
- The UI must label the destination **Controlled demo recipient**. We do not
  imply that it is a real hotel or hide that the recipient is automated.
- Judges may ask arbitrary information-gathering questions, but the receptionist
  answers only from a server-owned fictional fact sheet. Unknown facts remain
  unavailable.
- The existing human-only, exact-revision confirmation and one-attempt dispatch
  rules remain unchanged.
- Neither agent may book, purchase, cancel, accept fees, collect credentials, or
  make a commitment.
- The calling agent delivers its normal AI/transcription disclosure. The demo
  receptionist acknowledges it naturally and identifies itself as an automated
  test desk.
- The demo line is private infrastructure. Judges trigger it through CallBridge;
  its telephone number is not displayed or published.
- The receiver is isolated from the stable outbound session. A receiver fault
  cannot silently change the calling agent's authority, result contract, or
  retry behavior.
- Before purchasing any additional number, show the exact monthly price and
  expected per-minute charges. One temporary number is acceptable only as
  challenge infrastructure, not as a per-country user requirement.
- Receiver admission is atomic and fail-closed: at most one concurrent receiver
  session and 20 signature-valid Twilio inbound attempts per UTC day. Count
  validated attempts before returning streaming TwiML, including caller,
  destination, admission, and runtime rejections; reset only at 00:00 UTC.
  Invalid-signature HTTP traffic is isolated behind a separate edge rate limit
  and cannot exhaust the Twilio-call quota. A missing counter, budget state, or
  configuration rejects the call.
- Both provider legs have a 180-second hard maximum and a challenge-owned spend
  ceiling. Reaching either limit disables new demo admissions until an operator
  explicitly re-enables them.
- The final video must still show one real result, not only architecture or test
  controls.

## Premises

1. The controlled hotel is a recipient fixture, not CallBridge's market wedge.
2. A real PSTN hop matters because “the web has APIs; the rest of the world has
   phone numbers” is the product's memorable claim.
3. Transparency makes the demo stronger: judges should know the receiver is an
   AI and then judge whether CallBridge can converse with it correctly.
4. The receptionist's fixed facts make correctness measurable while still
   allowing judges to formulate their own questions.
5. The proof is the observable loop: WebMCP plan, human confirmation, one real
   call, natural dialogue, accepted evidence, and ChatGPT-readable result.
6. A prior human canary may support credibility, but the controlled line is the
   repeatable judging and debugging path.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Scripted receiver inside CallBridge

Return predetermined events and evidence without a telephone call. This is the
fastest and most reliable approach, but it proves UI orchestration rather than
the real browser-to-world product. Rejected as the primary demo because judges
could reasonably describe it as simulation theater.

### Approach B: Dedicated real PSTN AI hotel receptionist

Assign one temporary controlled destination to a separate inbound voice route.
The normal CallBridge worker calls that E.164 number. Twilio ConversationRelay
handles the receiver's live speech recognition, speech synthesis, and interruption
events. One isolated Durable Object owns admission, the single active receiver
session, the closed fact lookup, and terminal cleanup. Both sides use real carrier
media, but only the outbound CallBridge side produces the user's task evidence and
result.

This reuses the current outbound call, confirmation, extraction, callback, and
timeout foundations without cloning their raw Media Streams and OpenAI Realtime
transport. The receiver is a smaller text-event protocol with separate routes,
state, limits, and tests, so a receiver fault cannot destabilize the proven caller.

### Approach C: Full adversarial call laboratory

Add scenario controls for IVR, noisy audio, voicemail, refusal, contradictory
answers, long silence, and multiple languages. This would become excellent
long-term regression infrastructure, but the controls and state surface increase
deadline risk and distract judges from the simple product story. Deferred until
after submission.

## Recommended Approach

Build Approach B with only three bounded, testable behaviors borrowed from C:

1. one supported question answered from an exact fact record;
2. a second supported question answered from a different exact fact record;
3. one explicitly unknown topic, which must remain unanswered rather than be
   invented.

The destination appears in the call brief as:

> Aurora Demo Hotel  
> Controlled demo recipient · automated test desk

The judge can edit the questions and shareable context but cannot edit the demo
destination. The five general inquiry tools remain unchanged. A sixth contextual
tool, `create_demo_call_draft`, is registered only when the signed-in page has no
active task and the server-owned demo feature is available. It accepts the judge's
objective, questions, shareable context, and result language; Convex builds the
complete safe contract and resolves `aurora_demo_hotel_v1` to the configured E.164
destination. The model never supplies the number, policy, disclosure, call language,
attempt count, or cost ceiling. The normal arbitrary-destination product remains
available outside the judge fixture.

### Receiver knowledge contract

The fictional fact sheet is versioned and server-owned. A compact initial set:

- check-in begins at 15:00 and the automated desk is available 24 hours;
- arrivals after midnight are accepted for held reservations, but the demo desk
  cannot create or modify a reservation;
- breakfast runs 06:30–10:30 and costs THB 450 per person;
- two accessible room types have step-free access and roll-in showers;
- small pets under 10 kg are allowed with a THB 800 cleaning fee per stay, which
  the caller may report but may not accept;
- on-site parking is unavailable;
- `renovation_noise_schedule` is an explicit unknown-topic fixture, not a known
  hotel fact.

The fact sheet is stored as `aurora_demo_hotel_v1` with separate `facts` and
`unknownTopics` maps. Each fact includes `factId`, deterministic matching phrases,
and one canonical `spokenText`. ConversationRelay supplies completed caller prompts
and converts server-sent text tokens to speech. The server tries deterministic
matching first; only an unmatched utterance reaches the existing low-cost OpenAI
text model as a closed-enum intent classifier. The classifier may return only a
known fact ID, a known conversation intent, or `unknown`. The server validates the
result and sends only the selected canonical `spokenText` to Twilio. OpenAI never
generates receiver speech or hotel claims.

The internal `lookup_demo_hotel_fact` request is:

```ts
{ utterance: string; factSheetRevision: "aurora_demo_hotel_v1" }
```

Its validated result is exactly one of:

```ts
{ kind: "fact"; factId: string; spokenText: string }
{ kind: "unknown"; topicId: string; spokenText: string }
{ kind: "conversation"; intent: "greeting" | "thanks" | "goodbye"; spokenText: string }
```

The immutable unknown response is: “I don't have verified information about
that in this controlled demo.” Any unknown fact ID, revision mismatch, classifier
timeout, or lookup output outside this union resolves to that response; corrupt
server configuration ends the session as `grounding_failure`. Free-form judge
questions are therefore mapped to a closed set of server-authored utterances
rather than answered with free-form generation.

The receptionist never invents a policy, confirms a reservation, requests
payment, or asks for sensitive personal data. Prompt text and caller speech are
never accepted as fact-sheet instructions.

### Call behavior

- Greeting: “Aurora Demo Hotel automated desk. This is a controlled test line.
  How can I help?”
- After the caller's disclosure: acknowledge briefly and continue.
- Answer the question asked, without dumping the entire fact sheet.
- If interrupted, stop and respond to the latest complete request.
- If a fact is absent, say so once and do not speculate.
- When the caller has no more questions, say goodbye and end naturally.
- English only for the submission recipient. Romanian and Russian receiver voices
  are explicitly post-submission work.
- A Durable Object alarm and an authenticated Twilio Calls API update both enforce
  the 180-second maximum; a receiver WebSocket close is terminal and never redials.

### Implementation contract

#### Routes

- `POST /demo-hotel/voice` accepts the Twilio inbound webhook. It validates
  `X-Twilio-Signature` against the exact public URL and form body, then validates
  `CallSid`, `From`, and `To` before atomically consuming the one pending
  `DemoHotelAdmission` reservation.
- `GET /demo-hotel/session/:callSid/:streamNonce` is the ConversationRelay
  WebSocket used in returned `<Connect><ConversationRelay>` TwiML. The nonce is
  random, stored only as a hash, expires
  after 60 seconds, and is consumed on the first accepted upgrade.
- `GET /internal/demo-hotel/health` requires `DISPATCH_API_KEY` and returns only
  readiness booleans, active/remaining counters, fact-sheet revision, and the
  last terminal reason. It never returns phone numbers or provider identifiers.

#### Configuration

- `DEMO_HOTEL_ENABLED=false` by default.
- `DEMO_HOTEL_NUMBER` and `DEMO_HOTEL_EXPECTED_FROM` are secret deployment
  variables and valid E.164 values.
- `TWILIO_AUTH_TOKEN` verifies the inbound webhook and WebSocket handshake; the
  existing least-privilege control key performs the terminal hangup.
- `DEMO_HOTEL_MAX_CONCURRENT=1`, `DEMO_HOTEL_DAILY_ATTEMPT_LIMIT=20`,
  `DEMO_HOTEL_MAX_CONNECTED_SECONDS=180`, and
  `DEMO_HOTEL_MAX_DAILY_COST_MINOR_UNITS` are server-owned limits.
- `DEMO_HOTEL_MAX_RESERVED_COST_PER_CALL_MINOR_UNITS` is the conservative
  worst-case receiver cost reserved before a call can be admitted.
- `DEMO_HOTEL_FACT_SHEET_REVISION=aurora_demo_hotel_v1` binds every receiver
  session to one immutable fact revision.

#### State and idempotency

When the normal outbound worker dispatches an exact confirmed revision to the
Aurora preset, it first creates one `DemoHotelAdmission` containing the internal
`taskId`, `attemptId`, execution revision, expected `From`/`To`, fact-sheet
revision, reserved receiver cost, creation time, and a 120-second expiry. Only one
pending or active admission can exist. Creating the pending reservation and
deducting `DEMO_HOTEL_MAX_RESERVED_COST_PER_CALL_MINOR_UNITS` from the daily
receiver budget happen in the same atomic Durable Object transaction. Definite
rejection of outbound provider call creation cancels the admission and releases
the reserve. Provider-creation uncertainty retains the reserve until admission,
expiry, or reconciliation and never authorizes a retry.

After Twilio signature validation, the inbound webhook atomically consumes the
single matching, unexpired `From`/`To` reservation and binds it to the inbound
`CallSid`. Without that cost-backed reservation the route returns provider
`<Reject>` with no media stream or spoken response.
This correlates the receiver with the exact human-confirmed outbound attempt
without sending a secret through PSTN. The existing destination-busy gate rejects
a second judge flow before confirmation; the UI says the controlled line is busy
and can be retried after the active call ends.

One logical receiver session then exists per inbound Twilio `CallSid`:

`received -> admitted -> stream_bound -> active -> ending -> ended | failed`

Signature failure, caller/destination mismatch, disabled state, exhausted limits,
or budget failure terminates as `rejected` before Realtime connection. Duplicate
webhooks for the same `CallSid` return the same TwiML only while the nonce is
unused; they never increment counters or create another session. A second media
upgrade, reconnect, or mismatched `CallSid` is rejected. Any media disconnect
after binding is terminal for the receiver; there is no automatic reconnect or
redial. The terminal state and reason are persisted for 24 hours, then removed by
alarm.

#### Result and provenance

The receiver never writes the user's result. The existing outbound worker remains
authoritative and publishes the existing `InquiryWorkerResultCallback`:

- `taskId`, `attemptId`, `resultKey`;
- `actualCostMinorUnits` and `costStatus`;
- `InquiryCallResult.executionRevision`, `outcome`, `summary`, per-question
  `status`, `value`, `sourceEventId`, and `sourceExcerpt`;
- `unresolvedQuestionIds`, `durationSeconds`, `disclosureStatus`,
  `commitmentSafety`, `terminalReason`, and `terminalAt`.

The real PSTN proof is the conjunction of outbound provider acceptance, a signed
`connected` event, accepted answer evidence, terminal callback, and provider cost
reconciliation. Provider call IDs and the destination number remain restricted
operational metadata, not public evidence.

#### Failure mapping

| Receiver condition | Receiver action | Outbound/user-visible result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disabled, limit or budget exhausted | Provider `<Reject>`, no media or speech | `no_answer`; no retry or unreserved receiver cost |
| Invalid signature | Edge-rate-limited HTTP 403, no TwiML | No admitted receiver session or Twilio-quota debit |
| Caller, destination, or admission mismatch | Provider `<Reject>`, no media or speech | Not a valid judge fixture; no unreserved receiver cost |
| ConversationRelay or classifier failure | Canonical unavailable reply when safe, otherwise provider hangup; persist exact reason | `failed` or `partial`; evidence already heard is retained |
| Unsupported or prompt-injected topic | Canonical unavailable response | Question remains `not_answered` or `ambiguous` |
| Media silence/timeout | Provider hangup at bounded timer | `partial` or `failed`; no invented answer |
| Duplicate webhook or stream | Return same unused TwiML or reject consumed nonce | One logical receiver session per `CallSid` |
| 180-second deadline | Calls API hangup plus local terminal state | `connected_timeout`; no retry |

`summary` and every reported answer value are translated by the existing strict
extraction request into `contract.languages.result`; evidence excerpts remain the
exact provider-language quotes. This feature does not introduce a new translation
field.

### Isolation and abuse controls

- One singleton receiver Durable Object combines the admission gate, daily counters,
  and the only active ConversationRelay session; it remains separate from `CallSession`.
- Verify Twilio webhook signatures before returning streaming TwiML.
- Accept the demo flow only for the configured destination, expected CallBridge
  caller ID, and matching cost-backed admission; reject every other call with
  provider `<Reject>` before media or speech.
- Keep the destination number out of page source, application logs, screenshots,
  the public repository, and public WebMCP result text. Restricted provider-side
  call records necessarily retain routing metadata.
- Enforce the concrete atomic counters and spend limits above. Configure a
  provider/subaccount spending guard where available and alert on every rejected
  direct call or 80% budget usage.
- In the same transaction that creates the pending admission, atomically reserve
  one worst-case three-minute receiver allowance derived from the verified account
  quote. Definite outbound-creation rejection releases it. Provider uncertainty
  keeps it charged against the daily cap until UTC reset. Exact receiver-side
  settlement is deferred; the judging-window ceiling is enforced conservatively
  from admission count multiplied by the verified worst-case allowance.
- Do not retry a receiver or outbound session automatically after an uncertain
  provider outcome.
- Do not mix the receiver's internal Realtime usage into the user's call quote;
  label it as challenge-owned sandbox infrastructure in internal accounting.
- Number secrecy is not an authorization control. Disable the feature flag before
  number rotation or release, verify no active session, remove the inbound route,
  then release the number only after the judging window.

### Data handling

- No audio is recorded by CallBridge.
- Caller audio and text are forwarded only during the active receiver session.
- The receiver persists no raw caller transcript or shareable context. It stores
  only `CallSid` hash, fact-sheet revision, returned `factId` values, timestamps,
  admission counters, and terminal reason for 24 hours.
- The normal outbound worker keeps only its existing bounded evidence excerpts
  and scrubs raw Realtime turns after result preparation.
- Twilio and OpenAI may retain restricted provider metadata under their configured
  service policies; those records are not copied into WebMCP, public Activity, or
  the repository.
- An operator can disable the route immediately; the 24-hour alarm removes
  receiver session metadata without affecting the user's normal task receipt.

### Required tests

- valid and invalid Twilio signatures against the exact public URL/body;
- caller mismatch, destination mismatch, disabled flag, malformed form input;
- duplicate webhook idempotency, consumed nonce, direct WebSocket access, and
  reconnect rejection;
- missing, expired, mismatched, duplicate, and consumed outbound admission
  reservations; definite provider rejection cancels the reservation and uncertain
  creation does not;
- concurrent admission at the limit and atomic UTC daily-attempt exhaustion;
- missing/corrupt budget state and 80% alert threshold;
- prompt injection, unsupported topics, fact lookup, and exact unavailable reply;
- every receiver text token equals the canonical text selected by a validated
  lookup; no model-generated receiver claim is sent to ConversationRelay;
- no repeated resolved outbound question across two supported facts;
- ConversationRelay failure, classifier timeout, socket close, initial silence,
  interruption, and partial dialogue;
- authoritative provider hangup at 180 seconds;
- no number, raw transcript, provider ID, credentials, or private context in
  public logs, WebMCP output, Activity, result, or fixtures;
- exact result/provenance fields and provider-cost reconciliation;
- atomic worst-case cost reservation, proven-unused release, and full-reserve hold
  when actual cost is unknown;
- preflight readiness without placing a call.

### Judging-window preflight

The protected health check passes only when the feature flag, number topology,
webhook configuration, fact revision, Twilio control credentials, OpenAI
connectivity, remaining daily attempts, remaining spend, and receiver concurrency
are ready. Any unknown value makes readiness false. The release script checks
this endpoint before each canary and before filming; it never enables or places a
call.

## Success Criteria

- A judge can ask two original supported questions and one question about the
  explicit unknown-topic fixture.
- WebMCP creates the exact visible plan; no call exists before webpage confirmation.
- Exactly one outbound provider call and one receiver session are created.
- The receiver consumes the short-lived admission created for the exact confirmed
  outbound attempt; direct inbound calls cannot open a Realtime session.
- Both sides exchange real carrier media and complete the AI disclosure.
- The calling agent does not repeat a question after its answer is resolved.
- One unknown fact is reported as unanswered or uncertain, never guessed.
- The call ends naturally within 180 seconds.
- The result shows every question's status, accepted evidence, translated summary,
  duration, disclosure state, terminal state, and one-attempt receipt.
- ChatGPT retrieves and explains the completed result through WebMCP.
- Two consecutive unchanged canaries pass without manual repair, repeat dialing,
  or hidden retries.
- The complete sequence is understandable in the first 15 seconds of the video
  and fits in a public video shorter than three minutes.

3. **[P1] (confidence: 10/10) Provider topology is a release gate.**

5. **[P1] (confidence: 8/10) The receiver model classifies; it never authors hotel

6. **[P1] (confidence: 9/10) Provider hangup is authoritative.** Twilio documents

### Deadline architecture

```text
ChatGPT browser
  └─ create_demo_call_draft(objective, questions, context, resultLanguage)
       └─ Convex constructs exact server-owned InquiryCallContract
            └─ human confirms exact execution revision on webpage
                 └─ dispatch claim
                      ├─ DemoHotelRecipient.admit(attempt + revision + From/To)
                      └─ existing CallSession dials Twilio destination
                           └─ POST /demo-hotel/voice [signed]
                                └─ singleton DemoHotelRecipient consumes admission
                                     └─ <Connect><ConversationRelay>
                                          └─ signed WebSocket + one-use nonce
                                               ├─ prompt text
                                               │    ├─ deterministic match
                                               │    └─ closed-enum classifier fallback
                                               ├─ canonical fact text → Twilio TTS
                                               └─ interrupt/end/error → state transition

Existing CallSession hears the canonical answer over PSTN
  └─ existing evidence extraction + signed callbacks
       └─ Convex result
            └─ get_call_result → ChatGPT explanation
```

The receiver state machine is deliberately small:

```text
disabled
   │ operator enable + healthy configuration
   ▼
idle ── admit(exact confirmed attempt + budget) ──▶ reserved
 ▲                                                   │ signed matching webhook
 │ definite provider rejection                      ▼
 │                                                admitted
 │                                                   │ signed one-use WS
 │                                                   ▼
 │                                                 active
 │                                                   │ goodbye | error | 180s
 │                                                   ▼
 └──────────── terminal persisted for 24h ◀──────── ending

Any mismatch ──▶ rejected (no ConversationRelay session, no retry)
```

### Performance review

1. **[P1] (confidence: 8/10) Bound classifier latency.** Deterministic rules run
   first. Only one classifier request may be in flight; it receives a 1.5-second
   deadline and returns canonical unknown on timeout. No retries inside a call.

2. **[P2] (confidence: 8/10) Avoid Durable Object hibernation complexity.** A demo
   call is capped at 180 seconds and concurrency is one. Standard WebSocket state is
   cheaper in engineering risk than restore/attachment logic for this deadline.

3. **[P2] (confidence: 9/10) Bound persistence.** Persist state transitions, fact
   IDs, counters, and terminal reason only; never write prompt-by-prompt transcripts
   or audio. The single object makes N+1 database access inapplicable.

4. **[P1] (confidence: 10/10) Price before enablement.** The current account quote
   supplies the three-minute worst-case allowance. Unknown pricing keeps the health
   endpoint false and the feature disabled.

### NOT in scope

- Raw receiver Media Streams plus a second OpenAI Realtime socket — duplicates the
  proven caller transport and cannot be justified before the deadline.
- Pre-generated G.711 asset pipeline — canonical server text through provider TTS
  gives the same grounding boundary with far less code.
- Multilingual receiver — English-only judging fixture; general outbound calls keep
  their existing language support.
- IVR/noise/voicemail/contradiction scenario laboratory — valuable regression work,
  not necessary for the winning three-minute story.
- Exact receiver-side post-call settlement — conservative admission allowance caps
  spend through judging; full reconciliation is post-submission.
- A provider abstraction or second telephony vendor — no product value for this
  controlled fixture.
- SMS, WhatsApp, Telegram, and iMessage — separate roadmap channels.
